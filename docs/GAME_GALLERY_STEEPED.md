## Concept

**Setting** — A single quiet evening inside Auntie Mae's back-alley tea room, where a brass kettle on the counter dispenses colored glass orbs into a shallow display case below the window. Outside it is raining; inside, a record skips gently between hands of play.

**Mood** — Cozy, unhurried, and quietly focused — the feeling of arranging a windowsill on a rainy afternoon while a jazz piano trio plays a slow standard two rooms away.

**Art style** — Pixel art at 320x180, warm wood-grain background with visible knots and a brass-trim display case, glass orbs rendered with a two-pixel specular highlight, all UI numerals and labels hand-lettered in a slightly uneven serif as if chalked on a sandwich board.

**Core mechanics:**
  - Glass orbs in four tea-themed colors (chamomile gold, hibiscus red, matcha green, oolong amber) drop one at a time into a 6-wide, 10-tall display case that the player shifts left/right and rotates in pairs.
  - Clear a horizontal row by filling it entirely with orbs of a single color — mixed-color rows do not clear, rewarding patient sorting over speed.
  - Three difficulty modes — Loose Leaf (slow fall, preview of next 3 orbs, no game over), House Blend (standard fall speed, preview of next 1, top-out ends the session), and Bitter Steep (fast fall, no preview, color-blind orbs every 10th drop).
  - A 'steep' meter fills as same-color rows clear consecutively; topping it off triggers a one-time 'Pour' that drains the bottom row of mismatched orbs.
  - Single-session scoring: every game ends when the case tops out or the kettle runs dry at 100 orbs, with a hand-lettered receipt tallying rows cleared, longest combo, and tea earned.

## QA Report

**Verdict:** playable  ·  **Score:** 7/10

> Steeped is a charming, well-conceived cozy puzzle with strong narrative cohesion, deep aesthetic polish in the style guide, and warm NPC writing. The world, characters, and mood are production-ready. The major blockers are mechanical: the single-color row clear rule is statistically near-impossible on a 6-wide grid with 4 colors absent kettle RNG biasing, and the orb-drop verb (single vs. pair, rotation) is internally contradictory. The steep meter combo definition is ambiguous, and quest branching gates the Pour mechanic behind only one of three modes. None of these are unrecoverable — clarifying the drop unit, biasing the RNG, and unifying the combo rule would land the design. Quests, NPCs, items, and style guide are consistent and detailed; no critical narrative or content blockers were found.

### Issues found (12)

1. **[major]** Core clear rule is mathematically punishing: a 6-wide row requires six identical-color orbs in one row, but with 4 colors and ~uniform distribution, the probability of any naturally completed row being single-color is extremely low (~0.1% per random fill). Combined with no row-shift after partial clears and the only rescue being a single Pour from a filled steep meter (which itself requires consecutive same-color clears), the steep meter is functionally unreachable in early sessions. The clear rule needs either color reduction per row, partial-row scoring, or color-bias from the kettle to feel achievable.
2. **[major]** Concept says orbs drop 'one at a time' but also that the player 'shifts left/right and rotates in pairs' — these are contradictory. Tetris-style rotation requires pairs/pieces; single-orb drops cannot rotate. The rotation verb has no defined input target.
3. **[major]** Steep meter fill condition is underspecified. 'Consecutive same-color row clears' is ambiguous — does it mean clears of the same color as each other, or just consecutive clears of any single color? Combo-break conditions also unclear: does a non-clear drop (which is almost every drop) break the combo, or only a mixed top-out? quest_steady_hands requires three different colors cleared 'in a row' which conflicts with a 'same-color' interpretation.
4. **[minor]** quest_steady_hands has failure_condition 'case_top_out_before_third_clear' but is located in House Blend mode — players who pick Loose Leaf cannot top out, and the branch from quest_first_pot only routes house_blend to this quest. Players who pick Bitter Steep get routed away from steady_hands and thus cannot unlock quest_the_pour, despite the Pour being a core advertised mechanic.
5. **[minor]** quest_patient_pot has time_limit_turns: 100 but no failure_conditions and explicitly states 'no game over.' Loose Leaf disables game over, so a turn limit is contradictory. The 100-turn cap likely just mirrors the kettle capacity but reads as a fail timer.
6. **[minor]** obj_choose_mode uses objective_type 'talk' targeting item_mode_chalkboard, but chalkboard is an item not an NPC. obj_use_preview also uses 'talk' on item_preview_card. These should be 'interact' or 'use' type objectives.
7. **[minor]** item_pour_token is marked stackable: true with max_stack: 1 — stackable with a max of 1 is effectively non-stackable and inconsistent. Also, the design says the steep meter unlocks 'a one-time Pour' per session, so multiple tokens shouldn't exist.
8. **[minor]** Room layout has dimension and position conflicts. room_counter is at position [0,0] with width 2, room_display_case at [2,0] with width 6 (occupying x=2–7), room_window_nook at [8,0] width 1 — these fit horizontally. But room_record_corner is at [9,2] (outside 0–8 x-range) and room_receipt_spike at [0,2]. The composite layout implies an 8-wide room but elements extend to x=9. Verify whether positions are room-coords or world-coords.
9. **[minor]** npc_the_painter's location_id is room_display_case (a play_field, not a seating area). Painter is described as sitting closest to the case, but the case is the puzzle grid itself — there's no defined seating sub-area inside it. NPCs occupying the play field could visually conflict with orb rendering.
10. **[minor]** Bitter Steep introduces 'colorblind orbs every 10th drop' where oolong amber and chamomile gold are made visually indistinguishable. This is a difficulty feature, but it doubles as an actual accessibility hazard for colorblind players. The 4-color palette (gold/red/green/amber) also lacks shape differentiation — deuteranopia/protanopia users may struggle even outside Bitter Steep.
11. **[minor]** Record-skip is described as both a deliberate aesthetic beat ('between hands of play') and 'every third bar.' These are different cadences. If gameplay-driven, it must align with drop pacing; if music-driven, it ignores play rhythm. NPC barks (Rin, Tomas, painter) reference both interpretations.
12. **[minor]** No scenes_generated, scripts_generated, encounters, stat_system, or combat_system populated. The project is design-only — playability cannot be evaluated for an actual build. Marking 'playable' refers to design feasibility, not a runnable artifact.
